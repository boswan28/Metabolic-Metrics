Mission Statement

Why we advocate

Our goals at Metabolic Metrics are to inform people that it is never too late to begin making healthier choices, provide simple solutions to nutritional obstacles, and share recent, accurate research about nutrition and physical activity.

We aim to educate older adults on how they can improve their eating habits, become more aware of what they consume, and live more active lifestyles. By building healthier routines, older adults can support more stable energy levels and improve their overall quality of life.

Problem Statement

The nutrition and energy gap

Many older adults in the United States are not receiving the nutrients they need through their daily diets. Some rely on dietary supplements such as calcium and magnesium, yet many still experience nutrient deficiencies. At the same time, fast food and processed foods remain widely available because they are affordable, convenient, and easy to access.

This problem matters because processed foods are often lower in essential nutrients and can contribute to unstable energy levels. When older adults experience low energy, they may become less physically active, which can increase the risk of chronic disease, reduced mobility, and a lower quality of life.

Argument of Advocacy

What should be done

Claim of Policy

Federal, state, and local governments should expand nutrition education programs, increase funding for food assistance initiatives, support local farmers markets, and promote physical activity opportunities for older adults.

Evidence

Research from The American Journal of Clinical Nutrition found that about 8% of older adults in the United States experience food insecurity. Food insecurity can reduce access to important vitamins and minerals that support energy production, muscle function, bone health, and overall wellness.

Reasoning

Many older adults do not consume enough essential nutrients, including vitamins A, C, D, and E, calcium, and magnesium. When people depend on convenient processed foods, they may consume enough calories but still miss the nutrients needed for stable energy and long-term health. Improving access to nutritious foods, expanding nutrition education, and encouraging daily movement can help reduce these risks.

Farmers Markets

Supporting local farmers markets can help older adults access fresh, seasonal produce while encouraging movement and community engagement.

Nutrition Education

Communities should create more programs that teach people how to build balanced meals, understand nutrition labels, and maintain active lifestyles.

Food Transparency

Food corporations should be clearer about ingredients, nutritional value, and the health effects of processed products.

Counterargument

Some people argue that lower energy levels among older adults are mostly caused by aging, medical conditions, or medication use rather than poor nutrition or lack of physical activity. These factors can contribute to fatigue, but they do not eliminate the importance of healthy habits.

Rebuttal

Even though aging naturally affects energy levels, nutrition and movement still play a major role in quality of life. A balanced diet provides the body with essential nutrients, while regular physical activity improves stamina, mobility, joint health, and confidence. Promoting proper nutrition and consistent movement is a practical way to help older adults remain active and independent.

Call to Action

Policymakers, healthcare professionals, local organizations, and community members must work together to improve access to nutritious foods and wellness resources. Supporting food assistance programs, farmers markets, nutrition education, and physical activity initiatives can help older adults maintain their health, energy, and independence.

Our Values

What guides us

Healthy Aging

We believe every older adult deserves the opportunity to age with strength, care, and dignity. Healthy aging means maintaining physical and mental well-being so people can move through daily life with confidence and independence.

Independence

We value each person's ability to make choices about how they live. Consistent energy levels help older adults complete daily tasks, stay active in their communities, and rely less on assistance from others.

Evidence-Based Wellness

We value health decisions that are supported by credible research. Proper nutrition and regular physical activity are connected to improved stamina, reduced fatigue, and better long-term health outcomes.
