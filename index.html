Mission Statement

Our goals at Metabolic Metrics are to inform as many people as possible that it is never too late to make healthy changes, provide simple solutions to nutritional obstacles, and share recent evidence-based research.

We aim to educate older adults on healthier eating habits, increase awareness of nutrition, and encourage active lifestyles that promote stable energy levels and overall well-being.

The Problem

Many older adults in the United States are not receiving adequate nutrients through their diets. As a result, many rely on dietary supplements while still experiencing nutritional deficiencies. Processed foods and fast-food options have become increasingly common because they are affordable, convenient, and accessible.

```

These foods are often low in essential nutrients and may contribute to lower energy levels, poorer health outcomes, and reduced physical activity. When older adults experience low energy levels, they may become less physically active, increasing the risk of chronic disease, reduced mobility, and a lower quality of life.

```

Our Values

```

Independence

We value the ability of older adults to remain independent and active within their communities. Stable energy levels and healthy lifestyles help individuals perform daily activities and maintain confidence in themselves.
```

Our Advocacy

```

Policy Claim

Federal, state, and local governments should expand nutrition education programs, increase funding for food assistance initiatives, and promote physical activity opportunities for older adults.

Evidence

Research published in The American Journal of Clinical Nutrition found that approximately 8% of older adults in the United States experience food insecurity. This often results in reduced consumption of essential vitamins and nutrients necessary for energy production, muscle function, and bone health.

Reasoning

Many older adults do not consume adequate amounts of vitamins A, C, D, and E, along with important minerals such as calcium and magnesium. Improving access to nutritious foods, expanding nutrition education, and encouraging physical activity can help address these challenges.

Counterargument

Some experts argue that lower energy levels among older adults are primarily the result of aging, medical conditions, or medication use. While these factors certainly contribute to fatigue, research consistently demonstrates that improved nutrition and regular exercise can significantly improve energy levels, mobility, and overall quality of life.

Call to Action

Communities, healthcare organizations, and policymakers must work together to improve access to healthy foods and wellness resources. Supporting nutrition programs and community health initiatives can help older adults maintain independence and healthier lives.

```
